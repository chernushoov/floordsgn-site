Antimicrobial Hygiene Floor Systems

May 2026 · 7 min read

Hygiene-grade floor systems are the safety baseline for healthcare, food production, pharmaceutical manufacturing, and cleanroom environments. The hygiene specification combines four discrete elements: an inherent antimicrobial chemistry verified by ISO 22196, integral cove base eliminating wall-floor angle bacterial harbour, joint-eliminated monolithic surface, and a verification protocol with periodic re-test. Each element on its own is insufficient; together they form the IL Ministry of Health (משרד הבריאות) tender-grade hygiene specification. This page walks the four elements with chemistry options, system integration, IL channel, and the inspection regime for ongoing verification.

Element 1 of 4 — Chemistry

Antimicrobial Additive Chemistry

Three chemistry families dominate the antimicrobial-floor market. Each has different efficacy spectrum + durability profile + regulatory profile.

ChemistryMechanismEfficacy spectrumRegulatory profile
Silver-ion (Ag+)Slow-release silver ions disrupt bacterial cell membraneBroad — Gram+, Gram-, fungi, some virusesApproved internationally; food-contact safe
Polygiene (silver-chloride-based)Activated silver chloride releaseBroad — Sika certification for kitchen + hospitalEU + IL approved; HACCP integration
Triclosan (legacy)Lipid synthesis disruptionBroad bactericidalEU restricted use; phased out of healthcare
Zinc pyrithioneCellular metabolism disruptionBactericidal + fungicidalEU approved; industrial preferred
Copper-ion (Cu+)Copper oxidative disruptionBroad including some viruses (COVID-19 application)Approved; emerging in healthcare floors

For tender-grade IL hospital + food production: silver-ion or Polygiene chemistry is the standard, with copper-ion adoption increasing post-2020. Triclosan is increasingly excluded due to regulatory pressure.

Element 2 of 4 — Cove base

Integral Cove Base

The angle where wall meets floor is the single most failure-prone hygiene zone in any conventional flooring system. Tile + grout failure at this angle creates a bacterial harbour that's inaccessible to cleaning + accumulates organic matter. The integral cove base resolves this by forming a continuous curved transition from floor surface up the wall to 100 mm height with 30 mm radius — using the same resin chemistry as the floor body.

  • Material: Same resin family as floor (PU-cement / epoxy SL / MMA). Continuous chemistry eliminates cold-joint between floor + cove.
  • Height: 100 mm minimum; 150 mm preferred for HACCP critical control zones (cooking line, prep counter, wash zone).
  • Radius: 30 mm internal radius — meets HACCP cleanability standard. Smaller radius creates cleaning blind spot.
  • Termination: Tile or paint above cove with sealed transition. Caulked bead at termination boundary.

Cove base is non-negotiable for tender-grade hygiene flooring in IL. Health Ministry inspector rejects any installation without integral cove at all wall + column intrusions.

Element 3 of 4 — Joints

Joint Elimination + Replicated-Joint Sealing

Hygiene flooring's primary advantage over tile is joint elimination. The monolithic resin surface has no joints in the field area — joints only replicate substrate movement joints, and these are sealed with food-safe polyurethane sealant.

  • Field area: Zero joints across the project area. The resin pour is continuous.
  • Substrate-replicated joints: Where substrate has saw-cut or expansion joint, replicate through resin layer + seal with Sikaflex PRO 3 (food-safe variant) or equivalent.
  • Perimeter joint: At cove termination, joint between cove and wall finish — sealed with food-safe acrylic or PU. See expansion joints in resin floors.
  • Drainage detail: Floor drain flange flush or below floor surface; sealed transition. Drainage gradient 1:100 minimum.

For HACCP critical control points (slaughterhouse, dairy production, ice-cream filling), even substrate-replicated joints must be minimised — pour bays sized to eliminate field joints entirely (typical 4 × 4 m or smaller bays).

Element 4 of 4 — Verification

ISO 22196 Testing + Re-Verification Protocol

Hygiene chemistry effectiveness is verified by ISO 22196 — the international standard for measuring antibacterial activity on plastics and non-porous surfaces. The standard measures log reduction in bacterial population after 24-hour contact with test surface inoculated with reference organisms (Staphylococcus aureus + Escherichia coli typically).

Verification pointThresholdFrequency
Initial ISO 22196 certification at install≥ 2 log reduction (99% efficacy) for tender-gradeOnce at install — certificate attached to handover
Annual ISO 22196 re-test≥ 1.5 log reduction (efficacy degradation acceptable)Annually — required for HACCP compliance audit
Field swab testing (HACCP)< 10 CFU/cm² environment monitoringPer HACCP plan — typically weekly + after sanitation
Surface contamination after cleaning protocolVisual + ATP swabPer HACCP plan

Annual re-test is essential because antimicrobial additive can leach or degrade over time. A floor that meets ISO 22196 at install may drop below threshold at year 3-5 — requires re-coat or topcoat re-application.

Tender language for hygiene specification

Direct paste into BOQ for hospital, food production, or pharmaceutical projects.

"Floor system ISO 22196 certified with ≥ 2 log reduction against S. aureus + E. coli at installation. Antimicrobial chemistry: [silver-ion / Polygiene / copper-ion] integrated into [topcoat / body coat]. Integral cove base 100 mm height with 30 mm radius at all wall + column intrusions, formed in same resin chemistry as floor body. Zero field joints in resin floor; substrate-replicated joints minimised. Annual ISO 22196 re-test included in maintenance contract; re-coat triggered at < 1.5 log reduction. HACCP-compliant joint sealant chemistry [food-safe Sikaflex PRO 3 or equivalent]."

Common hygiene-spec mistakes

  • "Antimicrobial" without ISO 22196 certification. Marketing claim without measurable backing. Reject submissions citing "antimicrobial" without log-reduction data.
  • Cove base added as separate skirting after floor install. Cold joint between floor and cove creates bacterial harbour. Integral cove must be poured with floor.
  • Field joint left unsealed. Joint creates linear bacterial reservoir. Either eliminate (smaller pour bays) or seal with food-safe PU.
  • Annual re-verification skipped. Antimicrobial efficacy degrades; HACCP audit identifies the gap.
  • Cleaning chemistry incompatible with antimicrobial additive. Some hospital-grade disinfectants neutralise silver-ion. Verify cleaning chemistry per floor manufacturer spec.
  • Topcoat wear exposes non-antimicrobial body coat. Antimicrobial concentration only in topcoat — when topcoat wears, the protection wears with it. Specify integral antimicrobial through full body for high-wear zones.

Cost considerations

Hygiene-grade specification adds 15-25% to non-hygiene equivalent system cost:

  • Antimicrobial additive: +5-10% on resin material cost.
  • Integral cove base: +₪80-180 per linear metre (see tender BOQ template Line 9).
  • Joint elimination via smaller pour bays: +5-10% labour cost.
  • Initial ISO 22196 certification: ₪3,500-6,000 per project.
  • Annual re-test + maintenance: ₪2,000-4,000 per year.

For an 800 m² hospital corridor: hygiene-grade adds approximately ₪35,000-60,000 over baseline epoxy SL. This is small compared to the cost of single HACCP failure or healthcare-associated infection (HAI) liability claim.

Final read

Hygiene flooring is engineered, not added on. Antimicrobial chemistry, integral cove, joint elimination, and verification protocol all work together — removing any one element compromises the system. Specify all four in tender language, verify at install + annually thereafter, refuse installations that treat antimicrobial as a topcoat afterthought. Healthcare-associated infection costs are unbounded; hygiene flooring is the cheapest single control measure in the facility manager's toolkit.
