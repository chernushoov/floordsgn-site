# How to choose the right floor system

A practitioner's decision guide for Israel — residential, commercial, industrial, food, pharma, healthcare, sport and coastal use cases.

## Why this guide exists

A floor is the only building component that takes 100% of the load, 100% of the foot traffic, 100% of the chemical spills, 100% of the cleaning chemicals, and 100% of the visual judgement of every visitor. Pick wrong and you replace the floor in 3–5 years, lose certification, get sued for slip injuries, or shut the line down for re-coating during a CIP cycle. Pick right and you forget the floor exists for 15–25 years.

The wrong floor is rarely chosen because the right system was unknown. It is chosen because nobody walked the decision criteria in order. This guide does that walk.

## Section 1 — Decision criteria (universal)

Eleven criteria, in priority order. The first six are non-negotiable engineering constraints. The last five are economic and regulatory.

### 1. Substrate condition

Why it matters: every resinous and cementitious topping bonds to the substrate, not to itself. A failed substrate fails the topping above it. Substrate moisture above 4% CM (calcium-carbide method) or relative humidity above 75% will blow any vapour-tight epoxy off the slab within months. Pull-off strength below 1.5 MPa (ASTM D7234) means the system fails the substrate, not the chemistry.

- **New concrete (< 28 days):** moisture trapped. Forces you to either wait the full 28 days, or choose a vapour-permeable system — **Sikafloor PurCem 21/21N/22N** (water-based PU-cement, tolerates 6% CM and rising damp), or microcement on an epoxy-DPM primer (**Sikafloor EpoCem** moisture-tolerant primer). Excludes solvent-based epoxy SL.
- **Old concrete, sound (CSP 3–5 profile, > 1.5 MPa pull-off):** open canvas. Any resin system works. Diamond-grind to CSP 3–4, vacuum, prime (**Sikafloor-156** epoxy primer or **Sikafloor-161** for absorbent substrates).
- **Old concrete, cracked / oily / soft top-layer:** scarify deep (3–5mm), or full shot-blast + scratch coat. Soft top layers force a structural overlay — polymer-modified screed (**Sikafloor Level-25**) before topping. Heavy oil contamination demands MMA primer (**Sikafloor-13/14/15 Pronto**, methacrylate penetrates).
- **Existing ceramic tile:** keep tile only if hollow-sounding tiles < 10%. Mechanical key (diamond-cup grind tile glaze), epoxy primer with quartz broadcast (mechanical anchor) — then microtopping (2–3mm) or SL epoxy (≥ 3mm to bridge grout lines). Pushes toward **microcement + fiberglass mesh** (Ardex P82 primer + 4×4mm mesh in scratch coat) or **Sikafloor MultiDur ES-26** thick build.
- **Wood subfloor:** moves. Forces flexible system only — **Sikafloor ComfortFloor** PU SL (crack-bridging Class A4 / A5 per EN 1062-7) or rubber comfort-floor; never rigid epoxy.

**Choice push:** unknown or wet substrate → PU-cement; sound dry concrete → anything; cracked or moving → flexible PU or MMA.

### 2. Use case

The use case decides whether the floor is a finish, a piece of process equipment, or a safety device.

- **Residential:** finish. Aesthetics and tactility dominate. Microcement, decorative epoxy, terrazzo, parquet.
- **Office / retail:** finish + cleanability. Polished concrete L2–L3, vinyl-flake epoxy, microtopping.
- **Industrial light (assembly / warehouse picking):** durability. Epoxy SL 2–4mm, polished concrete L1.
- **Industrial heavy (forklift / racking / shock):** structural. PU-cement HD 9–12mm, dry-shake hardened concrete.
- **Pharma / cleanroom:** process equipment. ESD epoxy, coved seamless, vapour-tight, FDA-listed binders.
- **Food production:** process equipment AND safety device. PU-cement is the only correct answer for wet/hot zones.
- **Sport:** safety device + performance. Shock-pad ≥ 14mm + EN 14904 / IAAF / FIBA cert.
- **Healthcare / school:** safety + acoustic + low-VOC. Bacteriostatic resin (silver-ion), EC1+ / AgBB.
- **Outdoor / coastal:** weather + UV + chloride. Polyaspartic or aliphatic PU topcoat over MMA or PU-cement. Never aromatic epoxy.

**Choice push:** finish-only use cases tolerate everything; process-equipment use cases narrow the field to two or three SKUs.

### 3. Loads

Load is the single most common spec error. People specify "industrial epoxy" for a forklift floor — and lose the topcoat in six months.

- **Foot traffic only:** ≤ 1.5 mm any system. Microcement, polished concrete L1–L2, **Sikafloor-264** epoxy seal.
- **Wheeled equipment (pallet jacks, carts, hospital beds):** ≥ 2 mm + impact-resistant. **Sikafloor-263 SL** 2–3mm, polished concrete L1 with Li densifier.
- **Forklifts (electric, ≤ 3t):** ≥ 4 mm + roughness R10 maximum (R11 destroys PU wheels). **Sikafloor-263 SL** at 4mm with quartz-fill backbone, or **Sikafloor MultiDur EB-24** (heavy SL).
- **Heavy forklifts (LPG / diesel, 3–7t) and racking point-loads:** PU-cement 9–12mm. **Sikafloor PurCem 22N HD** (-40 to +120°C, point load ≥ 8 N/mm² compressive). Or dry-shake hardener with hardened-aggregate (Mastertop 100, Sikafloor-3 QuartzTop) into fresh concrete.
- **Heavy machinery (presses, stamping, drop-test rigs):** structural concrete with steel-fibre and machine-base isolation. No resinous topping survives direct impact > 50J.

**Choice push:** load class is the floor-defining variable for industrial. Get it from the operations manager, not the architect.

### 4. Downtime tolerance

The single criterion that eliminates 80% of products from the shortlist for retail, hospital and food retrofits.

- **Hours (overnight reopening, retail/pharmacy/hospital):** MMA only. **Sikafloor Pronto** family. Walk-on 45–90 min, full cure 2–3 h. Applies down to **-30°C** (cold-room repairs while the room is running).
- **One weekend (48–72 h, restaurant kitchens):** PU-cement fast-cure (Sikafloor PurCem 21N 6h walk-on at 20°C), or epoxy SL accelerated.
- **One week:** standard epoxy SL + topcoat. Sikafloor-263 SL + 304W gloss.
- **Open window (new construction, no operations):** anything. Terrazzo CS-31 (30+ days cure for full polish), Pandomo Loft (7-day full cure before sealer).

**Choice push:** downtime ≤ 8 hours → MMA, no exceptions. The price premium (40–60% over epoxy) is recovered the day operations don't stop.

### 5. Aesthetics required

A surface that performs but looks wrong is a failure for residential, retail, hospitality, automotive showrooms.

- **High-polish mirror finish:** polished concrete L4 (DOI > 80) with lithium densifier; terrazzo polished to 3000+ grit with **Sikafloor 305W** PU sealer; epoxy metallic FX 2-layer pour with carnauba wax.
- **Gloss but resinous:** **Sikafloor-264** topcoat gloss; **Sikafloor-305W** PU 2K gloss.
- **Satin (default for premium):** **Sikafloor 304W** PU 2K satin. Forgiving, hides micro-defects. Default for microcement.
- **Matte (loft / industrial-chic):** **Topciment Topsealer WT Matt**; polished concrete L1; raw PU-cement.
- **Textured (anti-slip mandatory):** broadcast quartz / silicon-carbide / aluminum-oxide; rubber.
- **Decorative — vinyl flake:** Sikafloor DecoDur EB-Flake (Torginol chip 1/16"–1/2", solid / Domino 2-color / Granite 3-color, coverage 40% light / 70% standard / 95% refusal).
- **Decorative — metallic FX:** Eckart Stapa Metallux pigments, 2-layer pour with solvent veil, technique-driven (Stonhard or trained applicator only).
- **Decorative — terrazzo:** **Sikafloor CS-31** cementitious 30–50mm Palladiana / **Sikafloor EM-10** epoxy 8–15mm.

**Choice push:** the higher the aesthetic bar, the higher the substrate-prep budget. Mirror-polish concrete that wasn't power-trowelled flat will look mirror-perfect every 30cm and rippled in between.

### 6. Slip class

Mandatory and quantitative. Germany's DIN 51130 (R9–R13, shoes) and DIN 51097 (A/B/C, barefoot) are the global reference; Israeli Standards Institute (SII) accepts them. Israeli Ministry of Health requires R10 minimum on commercial kitchens, R11 on wet-process food, R12 on butcher/fish/cold-room ramps.

| Class | Friction range | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| R9 | 6° – 10° | Dry interior (office, retail, bedroom). Sealed fine quartz. |
| R10 | 10° – 19° | General commercial, light kitchen prep, garage. **Default office and showroom.** |
| R11 | 19° – 27° | Wet kitchen, food packaging, supermarket back-of-house, car-park ramp. |
| R12 | 27° – 35° | Industrial food (meat / fish / dairy), brewery, vehicle wash, slaughterhouse. |
| R13 | > 35° | Saturated processes, ramps in food plants, abattoir floor under blood/fat. |

Barefoot (pools, showers): A (low slip risk), B (slope < 18°), C (slope > 18° + walking through water).

**Critical error:** R12 and R13 require **exposed broadcast** (silicon carbide 0.5–1.2mm or aluminum oxide 0.6–1.5mm, full broadcast to refusal ≥ 95% coverage). Sealing the broadcast with topcoat drops slip class by one or two grades. R13 with a clear topcoat is a marketing claim, not a reality.

**Choice push:** slip class determines broadcast media + density. Smooth = R9–R10 only. R12+ = saturated grit, no clear topcoat.

### 7. Chemical exposure

The chemistry of the spill, not the floor, decides.

- **None / mild domestic (water, soap, household pH 5–9):** anything. Microcement sealed PU 2K is fine.
- **Mild industrial (motor oil, hydraulic fluid):** epoxy SL **Sikafloor-263 SL** + **Sikafloor-264** topcoat. 24h spot resistance.
- **Acids — organic (lactic / acetic / citric from food):** PU-cement only. Epoxy hydrolyses under lactic acid above 30°C. **Sikafloor PurCem 21N / 22N**.
- **Acids — inorganic strong (HCl, H₂SO₄, HNO₃, battery/pickling):** vinyl-ester resin (Sikagard Wallcoat N-VE, Mapei Mapecoat I 24 W) or specialty Novolac epoxy (**Sikafloor-381**). PU-cement resists 10% HCl 24h, fails at 30%.
- **Alkalis (caustic NaOH, CIP cycles, concrete works):** PU-cement excellent. Epoxy SL good for cold caustic, fails on hot caustic > 60°C.
- **Solvents (acetone, xylene, MEK, toluene, fuels):** Novolac epoxy (**Sikafloor-381**, **Sikafloor MultiDur ES-26**) or vinyl ester. Standard bisphenol-A epoxy softens in ketones.
- **Oils (cooking, hydraulic, gear, lubricants):** PU-cement perfect — oleophobic by chemistry. Epoxy with PU topcoat acceptable for cold oils only.
- **Blood, fats, proteins (slaughterhouse, fish):** PU-cement HD with antimicrobial (Mapefloor Bioblock silver-ion) and R12 broadcast.

**Choice push:** as soon as the word "acid" or "CIP" appears in the spec, the answer is PU-cement. Period.

### 8. Temperature extremes

- **Normal (-10 to +40°C):** all systems.
- **Cold storage (-20 to -40°C):** PU-cement HD only. **Sikafloor PurCem 22N** rated -40 to +120°C. Epoxy goes brittle below -10°C. MMA possible (Pronto cold-cure) but coefficient of thermal expansion mismatches concrete in deep freeze.
- **Hot CIP / steam clean (+70 to +95°C continuous):** PU-cement HD mandatory. **Sikafloor PurCem 22N HD** at 9–12mm survives 95°C steam wash daily. Epoxy SL delaminates above 60°C continuous.
- **Thermal shock (freezer → wash bay, +90°C steam onto +5°C floor):** PU-cement HD only. Differential thermal coefficient between epoxy and concrete (3:1) shears the bond under thermal shock. PU-cement matches concrete CTE within 15%.

**Choice push:** any process with a wash-down hose hotter than 60°C, or a freezer/dock-leveler interface, or CIP cycles → **PurCem 22N HD, 9–12mm**, full broadcast, hot-coved 75mm radius to walls.

### 9. UV exposure

The single defect that kills aromatic resins.

- **Indoor, no windows:** any resin. Aromatic epoxy fine.
- **Windows / skylights (direct sun strips on the floor):** UV will yellow standard epoxy within 6 months. **Polyaspartic topcoat** (Sikafloor-2540 W, 2530 W) or **aliphatic PU** (Sikafloor 305W). Or accept the yellowing on a dark colour.
- **Outdoor:** never use aromatic epoxy. Polyaspartic topcoat (UV-stable, 24h cure, walk-on 4h) or aliphatic PU only. **Sikafloor PurCem 21** with **Sikafloor-2540 W** polyaspartic seal — full UV stable.
- **Coastal (Tel Aviv / Haifa / Eilat — Mediterranean salt aerosol):** aliphatic PU + saline-resistant primer. Add osmotic blister test pre-spec. **Sikafloor MultiDur EW-24** chloride-resistant.

**Choice push:** anything UV-exposed → polyaspartic or aliphatic PU. Aromatic epoxy is for sealed interiors only.

### 10. Cost — initial vs lifecycle TCO

The cheap quote is usually a 10-year-cost trap.

| System | Install ₪/m² (IL 2026) | Life | Re-coat cycle | Lifecycle ₪/m²/yr |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Painted concrete | 60–120 | 1–2 yr | annual | 60–80 |
| Epoxy SL 2mm + topcoat | 280–420 | 7–10 yr | topcoat at 5 yr | 50–70 |
| Sikafloor PurCem 21N 6mm | 480–680 | 15–20 yr | none | 35–45 |
| Sikafloor PurCem 22N 9mm | 650–900 | 20–25 yr | none | 30–40 |
| Polished concrete L3 + Li densifier | 220–360 | 25+ yr | re-polish at 10 yr | 25–35 |
| Microcement 3mm sealed PU | 380–620 | 8–12 yr | sealer at 5 yr | 60–90 |
| Terrazzo epoxy 12mm | 750–1200 | 30+ yr | re-polish at 15 yr | 35–50 |
| MMA Pronto 4mm | 580–780 | 12–15 yr | none | 45–60 |
| Rubber (EPDM, prefab) | 320–500 | 10–15 yr | none | 30–50 |

**Choice push:** for industrial and food, the cheapest 10-year cost is almost always PU-cement, despite a 2× higher install price than epoxy SL. For residential, polished concrete is the lifetime-cost winner if the slab quality permits.

### 11. Standards compliance

Standards are pass/fail gates. Picking a system that doesn't carry the certificate is automatic disqualification at audit.

- **HACCP / FSSC 22000 (food safety):** seamless, coved, drainable, cleanable. PU-cement family carries HACCP via ISEGA, NSF/ANSI 51, FDA 21 CFR 175.300. Sikafloor PurCem 21N, BASF Ucrete UD200, Flowcrete Flowfresh.
- **GMP / pharma (PIC/S, EU GMP Annex 1):** seamless, no joints in cleanroom, coved 75–100mm, no shedding, ESD optional. Sikafloor MultiDur EW-24 + Sikafloor ESD topcoat, or PU-cement coved.
- **ESD (IEC 61340-5-1):** surface resistance 10⁶–10⁹ Ω. **Sikafloor-235 ESD** (dissipative, carbon-black filled) or **Sikafloor-262 AS** (conductive with copper grid). Earth-bond every 40m².
- **FDA (food contact, indirect):** binder must comply with 21 CFR 175.300. PurCem, UCRETE, most epoxy-Novolac systems carry the listing.
- **EU 10/2011 (food-contact migration):** ISEGA tested. PU-cement family standard.
- **Israeli Kosher / Badatz:** PU-cement carries Kosher cert for dairy/meat separation lines.
- **EN 1177 (playground impact-attenuation, HIC < 1000):** fall height 0.6m → 25mm rubber; 1.0m → 40mm; 1.4m → 55mm; 1.8m → 80mm; 2.5m → 100–120mm.
- **EN 14904 (indoor sports surfaces):** shock absorption Type 1–4, ball rebound ≥ 90%, friction 80–110.
- **DIN 51130 / 51097:** slip classes (see Section 1.6).
- **EC1+ / AgBB (low-VOC, German schools/hospitals):** water-based PU 2K (Sikafloor 304W / 305W), microcement sealed water-based.

**Choice push:** read the spec, find the certifications required, eliminate every product that doesn't carry them. Don't argue. Auditors don't read sales claims.

## Section 2 — System recommendations by use case

For each case: recommended system + two alternatives + reasoning.

### 1. Tel Aviv penthouse apartment — residential, aesthetic, warm-floor

**Recommended:** Microcement 3mm in Pandomo Loft style, satin PU 2K sealer (**Sikafloor 304W**) over warm-floor heating (28°C max surface, ramp 2°C/day). Seamless wall-to-floor continuity if requested. Colour palette: Pampas PA-K4 / Stone PA-K6 / Sahara PA-T5.

**Alt 1:** European white oak engineered parquet 14mm, click or glued (Sika SikaBond-T55) on acoustic underlay over warm-floor — only if substrate is < 2% CM moisture.

**Alt 2:** Polished concrete L3 (1500-grit, lithium densifier **Sikafloor ProSeal W**) — requires good base slab; impossible if slab was screeded with thin self-leveller.

**Reasoning:** microcement is the only system that achieves seamless 200m² penthouse coverage with warm-floor compatibility and the aesthetic that buyers at this segment expect. Parquet has resale-value parity but is the wrong choice in a glass-wall sea-view penthouse where Tel Aviv humidity (75% RH summer) cycles wood movement. Polished concrete is best-in-class TCO but cannot be retrofitted onto a thin screed.

### 2. 200m² coastal villa in Herzliya — residential, sea air

**Recommended:** Polished concrete L2 (honed satin, 800-grit) with **Sikafloor ProSeal W** lithium densifier + **Sikafloor-2540 W** polyaspartic UV-stable seal. Chloride-resistant.

**Alt 1:** Decorative epoxy terrazzo **Sikafloor EM-10** 10mm with brass dividers — seamless, salt-resistant, aspirational.

**Alt 2:** Travertine or basalt natural stone — traditional, but grout will absorb sea-air chlorides and stain in 5 years.

**Reasoning:** coastal IL air carries chloride aerosol 3–5km inland. Aromatic epoxy yellows in UV-strip through windows. Polished concrete sealed with polyaspartic is the inertest surface you can install — no organic chemistry to break. Microcement is also valid but a 200m² villa is at the limit of microcement's 80m² seamless field, requiring control joints that compromise the seamless aesthetic.

### 3. Office floor 500m² in Ramat Gan — commercial

**Recommended:** Polished concrete L2 (DOI 40–69, satin) with lithium densifier and **Sikafloor 304W** PU 2K satin guard. R10 slip class.

**Alt 1:** Vinyl-flake decorative epoxy — **Sikafloor DecoDur EB-Flake** 1/8" Torginol Domino blend, 70% coverage.

**Alt 2:** Sikafloor-264 SL pigmented 2mm with **Sikafloor-305W** gloss topcoat — fastest install for tenant fit-out timelines.

**Reasoning:** offices are not industrial floors. Polished concrete is the lowest TCO at this scale, and the visible aesthetic ("concrete loft office") is the current tenant preference in Tel Aviv / Ramat Gan tech-corridor. R10 satisfies office accessibility law. Vinyl-flake epoxy is the alternative when the slab is not polishable.

### 4. Cafe + kitchen in Tel Aviv — small commercial + R11 kitchen

**Recommended dining area:** microcement 3mm sealed PU 2K satin (**Sikafloor 304W**) for the dining floor; **Sikafloor PurCem 21N 6mm** R11 broadcast (color quartz 0.7–1.2mm light coverage) for the kitchen, hot-coved 75mm to walls and equipment plinths. Transition: divider strip at the kitchen door, both sides flush.

**Alt 1:** Polished concrete dining + PurCem kitchen (same as recommended; concrete dining cheaper but less forgiving on spilled wine).

**Alt 2:** Epoxy SL dining (Sikafloor-264) + epoxy quartz-broadcast kitchen R11 — cheaper, but lifecycle is half. Skip this if the lease is > 5 years.

**Reasoning:** kitchens with hot oil, citric acid (lemon, vinegar), CIP detergents, and barefoot/chef-shoe slip risk are a textbook PU-cement zone. Epoxy in kitchens is the most-common spec mistake in IL hospitality — fails in 18–30 months under lactic-acid + hot water. The premium for PurCem (₪ +200/m²) is recovered in the first re-coat avoided.

### 5. Restaurant kitchen 80m² industrial — R12 wet

**Recommended:** **Sikafloor PurCem 22N HD 9mm**, silicon-carbide 0.5–1.2mm full broadcast to refusal (R12), hot-coved 100mm to all walls and plinths, radial gully drain at 1:50 slope. RAL 7037 Dust Grey or RAL 6011 Reseda Green (vegetable-prep visual zoning).

**Alt 1:** PurCem 21N 6mm + aluminum oxide 0.6–1.5mm medium broadcast — only for prep-only kitchens without heavy oil/blood.

**Alt 2:** BASF Ucrete UD200 HD 9mm — direct equivalent to PurCem 22N, equivalent specs.

**Reasoning:** R12 + hot wash + acids + oils + thermal shock from walk-in freezer to wok station = PurCem HD, no debate. The cove-base is not aesthetic, it is HACCP-required (no 90° internal angle that traps bacteria). Skip the cove and you fail the audit.

### 6. Warehouse 2000m² with forklifts — industrial heavy

**Recommended:** dry-shake hardener (Sikafloor-3 QuartzTop, RAL 7037 or natural grey) cast into fresh concrete + power-trowel + diamond grind L1 + lithium densifier **Sikafloor ProSeal W**. R10. Saw-cut joints at 6×6m, semi-rigid PU filler (Sikadur-31). Forklift lanes RAL 1023 yellow 100mm stripe.

**Alt 1:** Epoxy SL **Sikafloor-263 SL** 4mm pigmented + quartz-filled backbone — cheaper install, 8-year life vs 25-year for hardened concrete.

**Alt 2:** PurCem 21 6mm — overkill for dry warehouse, but justified if cold-room or chemical zones present.

**Reasoning:** forklift loads + pallet drops + the absence of acid/wash-down means hardened concrete beats every resinous topping on lifecycle cost. The error is putting epoxy in a forklift warehouse — first pallet-drop chips the topping, water gets under, delaminates progressively. R11 here would destroy PU forklift wheels (correct: R10 max).

### 7. Pharmacy storage room 300m² — GMP, ESD

**Recommended:** **Sikafloor-235 ESD** (carbon-black dissipative, 10⁶–10⁹ Ω surface resistance per IEC 61340-5-1), 2mm SL on **Sikafloor-156** primer, copper grounding strip every 40m², bonded to building earth. Coved 75mm. RAL 7035 Light Grey for visual control.

**Alt 1:** **Sikafloor-262 AS** (conductive, ≤ 10⁶ Ω) — for ATEX-rated zones with flammable vapours, electronics assembly. More restrictive earthing.

**Alt 2:** PurCem 21N ESD variant — only if wet-clean (rare in pharma storage).

**Reasoning:** ESD is mandatory at GMP-listed pharma. Standard epoxy is an insulator and will not pass IEC 61340 test. The choice between dissipative (235 ESD) and conductive (262 AS) depends on whether the room handles powders/aerosols (conductive needed) or finished tablets (dissipative sufficient).

### 8. Food production 1500m² — HACCP, CIP, R12

**Recommended:** **Sikafloor PurCem 22N HD 9–12mm**, silicon-carbide 0.7–1.2mm full broadcast R12, hot-coved 100mm to all walls, perimeter linear-slot drain at 1:80 slope to radial gully at 1:50. Zone colour-coded: RAL 6011 wash, RAL 5024 bottling, RAL 3009 oxide red meat, RAL 1015 ivory dairy, RAL 1023 yellow safety lanes (100mm).

**Alt 1:** BASF Ucrete UD200 / MF — direct competitor, equivalent HACCP cert.

**Alt 2:** Flowcrete Flowfresh HF — equivalent. Some IL importers stock Flowcrete only.

**Reasoning:** food production at this scale is a 20-year asset. The only systems that survive daily CIP cycles, lactic/acetic acid, ammonia (refrigeration leaks), blood/fat, and -40°C to +90°C thermal shock are PU-cement HD systems. The certs (HACCP, ISEGA, NSF/ANSI 51, FDA 21 CFR 175.300, Badatz Kosher) are all on the PurCem TDS. No alternative chemistry has all the certs.

### 9. Cold storage -20°C 400m² — MMA territory

**Recommended:** **Sikafloor Pronto cold-cure system** — Sikafloor-13 Pronto primer (mineral), Sikafloor-24 PurCem Pronto SL 4mm, Sikafloor-61 BC Pronto with 0.7–1.2mm broadcast, Sikafloor-53 TC Pronto pigmented topcoat. Applies at -25 to +5°C. RAL 5024 Pastel Blue (cold-zone industry standard).

**Alt 1:** PurCem 22N HD installed during cold-room shutdown (warm to +5°C minimum, install at +10°C, return to -20°C after full cure 7 days). Cheaper materials, hugely more downtime.

**Alt 2:** Bare hardened concrete sealed with **Sikafloor ProSeal W** — only for short-life or low-spec cold rooms.

**Reasoning:** MMA cures in cold. PU-cement and epoxy do not (epoxy ratchets cure speed at < 10°C; PU-cement won't cure < 5°C). The killer use case for MMA Pronto: shutting the cold room for 24 hours instead of 7 days. The ventilation requirement (strong methacrylate odour during cure) is the constraint — forced extraction + vapour PPE + occupancy return only after full cure + 30 min air exchange.

### 10. Gym / kids playground — rubber

**Gym recommended:** poured EPDM-PU system, 14mm shock-pad SBR base + 4mm EPDM wear-layer, PU-bound in-situ. EN 14904 Type 3 indoor sport. Granule colours: solid slate grey or 2-tone blend 60/40.

**Playground recommended:** poured EPDM-PU, thickness to EN 1177:
- 0.6m fall → 25mm
- 1.0m → 40mm
- 1.4m → 55mm
- 1.8m → 80mm
- 2.5m → 100–120mm

Multi-colour blend or graphic inlay (hopscotch, animals) in EPDM cuts.

**Alt 1:** Prefab rubber tile 50×50cm interlocking EPDM (Mondo / Domo) — faster install, replaceable damaged tiles, slightly lower seamless aesthetic.

**Alt 2:** Vulcanized rubber sheet (Mondo Sportflex Super X) — premium indoor sport, IAAF/FIBA cert, ~ 2× cost.

**Reasoning:** safety standards (EN 1177 HIC < 1000, EN 14904) are pass/fail. Specifying 9mm "rubber gym floor" is below code for shock-pad indoor sport — minimum 14mm. Specifying 25mm playground under 2m equipment is below code — minimum 80mm for a 2m fall. Don't argue with EN 1177.

### 11. Hospital corridor 800m² — bacteriostatic, easy clean

**Recommended:** **Sikafloor MultiDur ES-26** SL 2.5mm with **Mapefloor Bioblock** silver-ion antimicrobial, **Sikafloor-305W** PU 2K satin topcoat, coved 100mm. R10, EC1+ certified low-VOC, AgBB compliant.

**Alt 1:** Sheet vinyl (Tarkett iQ Granit / Forbo Sphera) — fastest install, lowest material cost, but seams at 2m intervals are bacteria reservoirs after 5 years.

**Alt 2:** PurCem 21N 6mm — overkill chemically, but ideal for ICU / theatre wash zones.

**Reasoning:** hospital corridors have low chemical load but high cleaning-chemical exposure (chlorhexidine, quaternary ammonium), high foot/bed traffic, and strict acoustic + low-VOC requirements. Silver-ion biocide reduces MRSA colonisation per published Mapei studies. Coved 100mm to wall — no 90° junction (HAI infection control).

### 12. Showroom car dealership — aesthetic + R10

**Recommended:** polished concrete L3 (1500-grit, DOI 70–79) with lithium densifier + **Sikafloor 304W** PU satin guard, R10. Metallic dispersed aggregate seed (**Sikafloor MetalTop** dry-shake during pour) for showroom premium effect.

**Alt 1:** **Sikafloor DecoDur Metallic FX** — Eckart Stapa Metallux 2-layer pour, dramatic depth, requires trained applicator.

**Alt 2:** Polished terrazzo **Sikafloor EM-10** 10mm with mirror-chip and brass dividers — ultimate luxury showroom, 3× the cost.

**Reasoning:** showrooms demand visual depth and gloss + tyre-mark resistance + R10 minimum (customers walking in heels). Polished concrete with metallic seed is the cost-optimum. Metallic FX epoxy is more dramatic but requires re-coat at 7–10 years; polished concrete is essentially permanent.

### 13. Coastal warehouse Haifa port — hot/saline

**Recommended:** **Sikafloor PurCem 21 6mm** (UV-stable formulation) + **Sikafloor-2540 W** polyaspartic UV-stable seal, R10–R11 aluminum oxide light broadcast, RAL 7037 Dust Grey. Chloride-tolerant.

**Alt 1:** Hardened concrete with chloride-resistant densifier (**Sikafloor ProSeal W** + Prosoco LSGuard) + polyaspartic clear seal.

**Alt 2:** PurCem 22N HD if forklifts > 5t.

**Reasoning:** port-side air is the harshest condition in Israel — chloride aerosol, UV from open dock doors, occasional rain wash through roller doors, hot summer slab temperatures. Aromatic epoxy yellows and embrittles. PurCem matches concrete CTE, polyaspartic topcoat survives UV indefinitely.

### 14. 50m² bathroom remodel — microcement

**Recommended:** microcement 3mm (2-coat) on **Ardex P82** epoxy 2K primer with 4×4mm fiberglass mesh, **Sikafloor 304W** PU 2K satin with anti-slip sand additive R10 (DIN 51097 Class B for barefoot shower zones). Same colour floor → wall → counter for continuity. Topciment Microbase + Microdeck system or Pandomo W3.

**Alt 1:** Large-format porcelain tile 80×80cm with linear-drain — easier maintenance, but grout lines compromise the "seamless wet room" aesthetic.

**Alt 2:** Resin epoxy 3D effect — gimmicky, dates fast.

**Reasoning:** bathroom remodels are why microcement exists. Wall-to-floor-to-counter continuity is the value proposition. Critical: fiberglass mesh in scratch coat (substrate-crack telegraphing is the #1 microcement defect), wet-zone PU sealer with anti-slip additive (DIN 51097 B minimum), continuous primer over old tile. Moisture max 4% CM before primer.

### 15. 100m² shop fit-out — microtopping or epoxy

**Recommended:** **Sikafloor DecoDur EB-Flake** 1/8" vinyl chip 70% coverage, **Sikafloor-305W** clear PU 2K satin topcoat. 4-day install, 7-day full cure. R10.

**Alt 1:** Microtopping 3mm (Topciment Microdeck) in Pampas PA-K4 or Stone PA-K6 with PU satin sealer — more premium aesthetic, 7-day install due to coat-cure cycles.

**Alt 2:** Polished concrete L2 — only if existing slab is < 5 years old, hard-trowelled, and undamaged.

**Reasoning:** retail fit-outs are downtime-sensitive (every closed day costs the tenant rent + revenue). Vinyl-flake epoxy is the fastest premium-look system; microtopping is the most premium-look but slower. Decision pivots on the brand brief and the lease's fit-out window.

## Section 3 — "What goes where": what NOT to do

The dark side of floor specification. Five expensive errors that an experienced spec writer never makes.

### 3.1 Why not epoxy in food (vs PU-cement)

Standard bisphenol-A epoxy hydrolyses under hot water + lactic acid + sodium hypochlorite (the CIP cocktail). Within 18–30 months the surface chalks, the broadcast loosens, the topcoat softens. Bacteria colonise the soft layer. Audit failure. Even Novolac epoxy (Sikafloor-381) is only acceptable in cold acid zones — not in hot kitchens or CIP'd dairies. PU-cement is chemically inert to lactic/acetic/citric/hypochlorite up to 80°C, matches concrete CTE so it doesn't shear under thermal shock, and carries HACCP / ISEGA / NSF / FDA / Kosher certifications on the same TDS. There is no economic case for epoxy in a food-production zone with wash-down. The only place epoxy belongs in food is dry-packaging zones with no hot wash.

### 3.2 Why not microtopping in heavy industrial

Microtopping is 2–3mm of polymer-modified cementitious overlay. It's a decorative finish, not a structural floor. A forklift wheel point-loads 4–6 kN over a 30cm² contact patch. Microtopping cracks at 1–2 MPa flexural; the point-load is 15–20 MPa. Pallet drops dent. Even with the most aggressive PU 2K sealer it scuffs within months of forklift traffic. Microtopping is for residential, retail, showroom, restaurant dining floors — never for warehousing, manufacturing, loading docks, or anywhere with wheeled equipment heavier than a service cart.

### 3.3 Why not polished concrete in pharma without sealing

Bare polished concrete is alkaline (pH 12–13 fresh, 9–10 cured). It absorbs moisture. It outgasses CaCO₃ dust under foot traffic ("dusting"). Without a lithium densifier the surface fails the surface-hardness Mohs 7 test required for cleanroom. Without a topical sealer the surface fails the cleanability test (chemicals absorb, then leach during cleaning). Pharma cleanrooms require seamless, vapour-tight, non-shedding, easy-clean surfaces — bare polished concrete fails all four. Either seal it (epoxy SL + ESD topcoat) or use a resinous system from the start. Polished concrete in pharma without densifier + sealer is a non-starter at GMP audit.

### 3.4 Why not MMA in residential

MMA monomer (methyl methacrylate) is highly volatile during cure. The cure odour is intense — "nail-salon × 10" — and persists 24–48 hours. Required PPE for applicators is half-face respirator with vapour cartridges. Ventilation is forced-extraction with isolation. In a residential apartment with closed neighbours, shared HVAC, and no industrial extraction, MMA cure odour will infiltrate adjacent units and complaints will follow. The product is brilliant in cold rooms, hospital wards (with isolation), supermarkets (overnight close). It is the wrong product in a Tel Aviv high-rise apartment. Use microcement, polished concrete, or parquet for residential.

### 3.5 Why not rubber in chemical lab

EPDM and SBR rubber bind via polyurethane — and polyurethane is dissolved by ketones (acetone, MEK), aromatics (toluene, xylene), and concentrated mineral acids. A chemistry lab with bench-top solvent spills will see the rubber matrix soften, swell, and lose granule cohesion within months. Rubber's correct domains are dry impact-attenuation (sport, playground, fitness, kids' rooms) and acoustic comfort. The right chemistry-lab floor is Novolac epoxy (**Sikafloor-381**) or vinyl-ester (Sikagard Wallcoat N-VE) — purpose-built for solvent resistance.

## Section 4 — Five-question decision tree

The text version of the decision-tool wizard logic. Walk these five questions in order and the system will narrow to one or two products.

### Q1 — What's the use case?

- **Residential** → go to Q2 (aesthetics dominate)
- **Office / retail / showroom** → go to Q2 (aesthetics + light traffic)
- **Industrial / warehouse** → go to Q3 (loads dominate)
- **Food / pharma / healthcare** → go to Q4 (chemistry + certs dominate)
- **Sport / playground** → rubber EN 14904 / EN 1177 (Section 2.10 thickness table). Stop here.
- **Outdoor / coastal** → go to Q5 (UV + chloride dominate)

### Q2 — What's the substrate and downtime?

- **New construction or full renovation, dry sound slab** → polished concrete L2–L3 if slab quality permits; otherwise microcement (residential premium) or vinyl-flake epoxy (commercial fit-out).
- **Existing tile floor, tenant fit-out, must stay closed ≤ 2 days** → microcement on epoxy primer + fiberglass mesh.
- **Existing tile, < 8 hours downtime** → MMA Pronto (Sikafloor-24 PurCem Pronto SL 4mm).
- **Wood subfloor** → ComfortFloor PU SL (crack-bridging) or engineered parquet (only if < 2% CM moisture).

### Q3 — What's the load class?

- **Pallet jacks / carts only** → Sikafloor-263 SL 2–3mm + Sikafloor-264 topcoat. R10.
- **Electric forklifts ≤ 3t** → Sikafloor-263 SL 4mm + 264 topcoat OR Sikafloor MultiDur EB-24. R10 max (R11 destroys PU wheels).
- **LPG/diesel forklifts 3–7t, racking point loads** → PurCem 22N HD 9–12mm OR dry-shake hardened concrete + Li densifier.
- **Heavy machinery > 7t, presses, drop-test rigs** → engineered concrete with steel-fibre + isolation pads. No resinous topping survives direct impact > 50J.

### Q4 — What's the chemistry + temperature + cert requirement?

- **Hot wash + acids + thermal shock + HACCP** (food production, brewery, dairy, meat, fish) → **PurCem 22N HD 9–12mm, silicon-carbide R12 broadcast, hot-coved 100mm, RAL food-industry palette.** Non-negotiable.
- **Wet kitchen + R11 + HACCP** (restaurant kitchen, cafe back-of-house) → PurCem 21N 6mm + aluminum oxide medium broadcast, hot-coved 75mm.
- **Cold storage -20 to -40°C** → PurCem 22N HD (warm install) OR MMA Pronto cold-cure (in-service install).
- **Pharma + ESD + GMP** → Sikafloor-235 ESD (dissipative) or 262 AS (conductive) on 156 primer, coved 75mm, earth-bonded.
- **Healthcare corridor + low-VOC + bacteriostatic** → Sikafloor MultiDur ES-26 with Bioblock silver-ion + 305W satin, R10, EC1+ certified.
- **Solvent lab (ketones, aromatics, strong acids)** → Novolac epoxy Sikafloor-381 OR vinyl-ester Sikagard N-VE.
- **None of the above (dry, no chemistry, no certs)** → Q5.

### Q5 — What's the UV exposure + aesthetic + budget?

- **Indoor only, no UV, premium aesthetic, residential** → microcement 3mm + Sikafloor 304W satin. Pandomo Loft style.
- **Indoor + windows / skylights, premium aesthetic** → polished concrete L2–L3 + lithium densifier + polyaspartic seal (Sikafloor-2540 W).
- **Indoor commercial fit-out, fast install, premium aesthetic** → Sikafloor DecoDur EB-Flake 1/8" Torginol Domino blend 70% coverage + 305W gloss.
- **Outdoor / coastal / port** → PurCem 21 + Sikafloor-2540 W polyaspartic OR hardened concrete + polyaspartic. Never aromatic epoxy.
- **Budget-constrained warehouse, dry, foot + light wheeled** → dry-shake hardener cast into fresh concrete + grind L1 + lithium densifier. Lifetime-cheapest industrial floor.

## Glossary (one-line)

- **CSP** — Concrete Surface Profile (CSP 1 smooth → CSP 9 scarified), ICRI standard.
- **CM** — Calcium-carbide moisture test, % mass moisture.
- **DOI** — Distinctness of Image, ASTM E430, the polished-concrete gloss measure.
- **R9–R13** — Slip class DIN 51130 (shoes), ramp angle at slip onset.
- **A/B/C** — Slip class DIN 51097, barefoot, ramp angle at slip onset.
- **HIC** — Head Injury Criterion, EN 1177 playground impact-attenuation.
- **CTE** — Coefficient of Thermal Expansion. Concrete ~10×10⁻⁶ /K; PU-cement ~12×10⁻⁶; epoxy ~30×10⁻⁶ (3× concrete → shears under thermal shock).
- **CIP** — Clean-In-Place. Hot caustic + acid wash cycle for food/beverage equipment.
- **SSD** — Saturated Surface Dry. The required substrate-moisture state for PU-cement.
- **L1–L4** — CPC Concrete Polishing Council polish-level matrix.
- **Cove-base** — Curved transition from floor to wall (R50/75/100/150mm) eliminating 90° bacteria-trap.
- **Refusal** — Broadcast applied until the resin can no longer absorb additional aggregate (≥ 95% coverage), required for R12/R13.
- **ISEGA** — German food-contact migration certification (EU 10/2011).
- **EC1+ / AgBB** — German low-VOC certifications for schools / hospitals.
