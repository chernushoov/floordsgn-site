## SECTION 1 — RUBBER FLOORS

### 1.1 Scope & Standards

Rubber flooring in IL falls into four very different families that share only the polymer base:

| Family | Form | Primary use | Key standard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Poured-in-place PU/EPDM | Liquid binder + rubber granules, cast on site | Sports halls, athletics tracks, playgrounds | EN 14904 (indoor sport), EN 1177 (playground impact), EN 14877 (outdoor sport) |
| Comfort PU | Self-levelling PU with elastic membrane | Residential, schools, hospitality | EN 13501 fire, IIC sound |
| Sheet rubber (calendered) | Factory-cured rolls/tiles | Healthcare, transit, education | EN 1817, ISO 26987 chemical |
| Rubber tiles (recycled) | Pressed SBR/EPDM tiles | Gyms, garage, terraces, playgrounds | ASTM F1292 (fall), ASTM F2772 (sports) |

Two safety standards everyone in IL spec-writing must know:
- **EN 14904** — indoor multi-sports. Classifies floors as **Point-Elastic (P1–P3)**, **Area-Elastic (A1–A4)** and **Combined-Elastic (C3–C7)** by shock absorption + vertical deformation. ([Mondo guide](https://www.mondoworldwide.com/na/en/spaziomondo/doyouknow/how-choose-indoor-sports-floor/), [Junckers EN 14904](https://www.junckershardwood.com/sports-flooring/specification-guide-for-sports-flooring-en-14904-fiba-and-more))
- **ASTM F1292 / EN 1177** — playground impact attenuation. Defines **Critical Fall Height (CFH)** — the maximum drop from which g-max and HIC stay under the life-threatening threshold (HIC < 1000). CFH is set by **shock-pad thickness**, not top coat. ([ASTM F1292 spec](https://store.astm.org/f1292-22.html), [Berleburger BSW](https://www.berleburger.com/en/playground-mat/quality-and-safety/))

### 1.2 Poured-in-place PU rubber (sports + playground)

**System anatomy** — always two layers cast wet on a primed concrete or asphalt base:
1. **Primer** — PU primer rolled onto cleaned substrate. Wear window ~10 min before granule mix must hit it. ([AdventureTurf install](https://adventureturf.com/article/the-poured-rubber-surfacing-installation-process/))
2. **Base / shock-pad** — SBR granules (recycled tyre buffings) + PU binder, ~10–12% binder by weight, screeded to thickness that targets the chosen Critical Fall Height. ([replaysurfacing DIY](https://replaysurfacing.com/diy-pour-in-place-rubber-surfacing/))
3. **Wear course** — pigmented EPDM granules + PU binder, 18–20% binder for UV/wear, troweled smooth to 10–15 mm.

#### Cost (IL estimates) `[verify]`

Sport-grade poured PU is project-quoted, but ballpark for IL (converted from EU spec + 15–25% import duty/install premium):
- Indoor multi-sport hall (Conica HG / Polytan): ₪450–₪750 / m² supplied-and-installed `[verify]`
- Outdoor athletics track (Polytan/Conica EPDM): ₪500–₪900 / m² `[verify]`
- Playground PIP (40–80 mm system, CFH ≤ 2.0 m): ₪380–₪650 / m² `[verify]`

#### Lifespan & recoat
- Sport poured PU: **15–25 years structural**, repaint/refresh **every 8–12 years** (depending on UV/use).
- Playground PIP wear course: **7–12 years** before EPDM granule loss requires re-broadcast or full re-cap.
- Standard supplier warranty: 5–8 years against wear/delamination.

### 1.5 Rubber tiles (SBR/EPDM, recycled rubber, gym tiles)

The IL gym/garage/playground budget tier. Two construction grades:

| Grade | Composition | Thickness range | Typical use | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pure SBR (recycled tyre)** | 100% SBR granules + PU binder, black + colour fleck | 8 / 10 / 12 / 15 / 20 / 30 / 40 / 50 mm | Home gym, garage, weight rooms | Cheapest. Smells for first weeks. UV-fades. |
| **SBR core + EPDM top** | SBR base + 2–3 mm EPDM colour wear layer | 15 / 20 / 30 / 40 / 50 mm | Commercial gyms, schools, playgrounds | EPDM top is UV-stable + cleaner |
| **100% EPDM** | Virgin EPDM granules + PU binder | 8 / 10 / 15 / 20 / 30 mm | Premium / colour-critical / play | Most expensive |

**Standard thicknesses & matching use** (industry-wide, from [greatmats EPDM gym](https://www.greatmats.com/how-thick-is-epdm-gym-flooring.php), [gym-flooring.com SBR vs EPDM](https://www.gym-flooring.com/blogs/news/sbr-vs-epdm-gym-flooring-which-should-you-choose)):
- 8–10 mm — cardio area, light fitness
- 12–15 mm — free weights up to 50 kg
- 20–30 mm — CrossFit, Olympic lifting from low height
- 40–50 mm — drop platforms, dropped barbells from overhead
- 50–100 mm — playgrounds, CFH 1.5–3.0 m

**Cost IL `[verify]`:**
- Recycled SBR 20 mm: ₪90–₪140 / m² material; +₪40–₪80 / m² install (interlocking → glue-down adds)
- SBR+EPDM 20 mm: ₪150–₪250 / m²
- 100% EPDM 15 mm: ₪220–₪400 / m²

**Install method:** Interlocking (puzzle-edge) for DIY/temporary; **glue-down with PU adhesive** for commercial — required by all manufacturer warranties.

**Lifespan:** 10–20 years; UV-exposed tiles fade in 3–5 years (cosmetic only).

### 1.6 Conductive / ESD rubber — server rooms, electronics, MRI

For ATEX zones, hospital MRI suites, semiconductor handling, server rooms — flooring must dissipate static charge into a grounded loop.

**Specification window** ([StaticWorx EC rubber](https://staticworx.com/products/flooring/ec-sd-rubber/), [Sika ESD](https://www.sika.com/en/construction/floor-systems/esd-flooring.html)):
- **Conductive (EC)**: resistance 1.0 × 10⁴ – 1.0 × 10⁶ Ω. Hard ATEX zones, EPA workstations.
- **Static-Dissipative (SD)**: resistance 1.0 × 10⁶ – 1.0 × 10⁹ Ω. Server rooms, control rooms, healthcare.
- Critical metric: **body voltage generation < 100 V** with required footwear; **< 400 V** for general office/server room. ([StaticWorx specifying](https://staticworx.com/learning_center/selecting-specifying-esd-floor/electronics-handling-manufacturing-applications/))
- Test methods: **ASTM F150** (resistance) and **ANSI/ESD STM 7.1**.

**System options:**
| System | Construction | Thickness | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sika Sikafloor ESD (epoxy/PU + conductive copper grid + ESD topcoat) | conductive primer + ESD epoxy + conductive copper-tape grid | 2–4 mm | electronics, ATEX, labs |
| Nora norament conductive tile | homogeneous rubber + conductive carbon black | 3.5 mm | server rooms, MRI suites |
| StaticWorx Eclipse EC rubber | conductive rubber tile + grounding strips | 3.5 mm | data centres, control rooms |

**Cost IL `[verify]`:** ₪450–₪900 / m² installed (the grid + grounding install is most of the cost).

### 1.7 Schools / kindergartens — safety standards stack

In IL projects for schools and kindergartens the binding triple is usually:
1. **EN 1177** — playground impact (CFH, HIC, g-max) ([Berleburger BSW](https://www.berleburger.com/en/playground-mat/quality-and-safety/))
2. **EN 14904** — indoor multi-sports if gym hall is in scope
3. **EN 13501-1** — fire reaction class (Bfl-s1 typical minimum for educational interiors)

**Practical sub-spec:**
- Indoor classrooms / corridors → 2–3 mm sheet rubber (Nora, Mondo Harmoni) or 2 mm safety vinyl (Polysafe), R10 minimum
- Indoor gym hall → 8–10 mm poured PU on elastic layer (Conica HG, Polytan), Class P2/P3 or A4
- Outdoor playground → SBR shock pad sized to CFH + 10–15 mm EPDM wear coat (Berleburger Regupol pattern)
- Kindergarten nap rooms → 4 mm Comfortfloor PS-66 for sound + softness

### 1.8 Healthcare — bacteriostatic, autoclavable

The hospital spec is owned by **Nora by Interface** at the global level. ([nora healthcare hygienic floors](https://www.nora.com/united-states/en/market-segments/healthcare/rubber-flooring-benefits/hygienic-floors))

Why Nora dominates:
- Dense vulcanised rubber — no plasticisers, no PVC, no halogens
- Chemical-resistance tested to **EN ISO 26987**
- Surface inhibits bacterial growth (no wax/sealer required) — passes hospital infection-control audits
- R9–R11 slip rating in standard rooms; R11–R12 in wet zones
- Conductive / SD versions for OR, MRI
- Welded seams give monolithic surface (cove the walls = no joints anywhere)

Alternative: **Altro safety floors** with welded seams + sit-on cove (for kitchens, sluice rooms, wet labs). ([Altro Stronghold 30 — commercial kitchens](https://www.altro.com/us/products/altro-stronghold-30))

**Autoclavable?** Bulk rubber sheet is **not autoclavable** — instead, the hygiene model is "monolithic surface + welded seams + cove-up walls + daily wet-clean with neutral detergent". This is the IL Ministry-of-Health expected spec for OR/ICU corridors.

## SECTION 2 — MICROTOPPING / MICROCEMENT MATERIALS

### 2.1 What this category actually is (and isn't)

"Microcement" is a marketing umbrella over several **chemically distinct** systems. Specifying one when you needed another is the #1 cause of failures the IL market sees.

Three binder families:

| Binder family | Examples | Strengths | Weak point |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cement + acrylic / polymer resin (2-comp)** | Topciment Sttandard, Pavistamp PAVICEM, Sikafloor MicroTop | Hard, abrasion-resistant, fast curing | Needs PU sealer for wet areas; can hairline crack on flex |
| **Cement + lime + polymer (hybrid)** | Mortex (Beal), Mineral Skin (Beal) | Naturally water-repellent, organic look | Softer than pure-cement, more sensitive to acid |
| **Pure lime** (no cement) | Marmorino, Veneziano, Tadelakt | Breathable, antibacterial, period-authentic | Slow cure (carbonation), demanding application, not for floors at high traffic |
| **Epoxy / PU pseudo-microcement** | Some "decorative concrete overlays" in US (Increte, Decra-Mix) | Chemically robust, can mimic look | Not breathable, not technically microcement |

Always ask the **TDS** for binder chemistry, **never** rely on the trade name.

### 2.3 Composition deep-dive

#### Cement-based 2-component microcement (the "modern" microcement)
Typical formulation ([Topciment composition](https://www.topciment.com/en-us/microcement)):
- **Hydraulic binder** — white or grey cement (often CEM I 52.5)
- **Selected aggregates** — fine quartz or limestone (granulometry: 0.1–0.9 mm depending on product)
- **Synthetic resin** — acrylic, styrene-acrylic, or hybrid polymer (component B, the "liquid")
- **Specific additives** — pigments, rheology modifiers, fibres, water-retainers

Cure: hydraulic + film-forming. Functionally cured 24–48 h, fully cured 7 days. Resin enhances flex, abrasion, and reduces shrinkage cracks.

#### Lime-only (Marmorino / Tadelakt)
- Slaked lime putty (Ca(OH)₂) + crushed marble / marble dust / very fine sand
- Pigments (mineral, lime-fast only — no synthetic dyes)
- Tadelakt also: olive-oil soap (saponified at the end with the lime → calcium stearate plug = waterproofing)
- Cure: **carbonation** with atmospheric CO₂ — slow, weeks to months for full hardness. Bacteriostatic by high pH during cure.

#### Mortex / hybrid
- Cement + slaked lime + acrylic resin + mineral pigment
- Mass-waterproof (no through-pores), but still slightly breathable thanks to lime

#### Béton Ciré (French)
- White or grey cement + ground stone aggregate + acrylic/PU resin + mineral pigment
- Two coats × ~1.5 mm, often sanded between coats, then PU or wax sealed

### 2.5 Waterproofness — single most-asked question

**Headline:** Microcement is **water-repellent**, not **waterproof in code-compliant sense**. For a wet room (IL shower / bathroom / pool / spa), the build-up must be:

1. **Substrate** — sound concrete, screed, or tile
2. **Waterproof membrane** — code-compliant tanking (cementitious or liquid PU/polymer) under the microcement; sealing strips at floor-wall junction
3. **Reinforcement mesh** in corners and stress points
4. **Microcement base + finish coats** — 2–3 mm total
5. **PU sealer** — 2 coats, with 7-day cure before water contact in showers ([festfloor microcement bathroom](https://festfloor.com/pages/microcement-in-the-bathroom))

The microcement layer is the **decorative + abrasion** layer; the membrane below is the **water-stop**. ([Resin Flooring Co. waterproofing microcement](https://www.resinflooringcompany.com/waterproofing-and-microcement-within-a-bathroom-or-wetroom/), [Smartcret problems guide](https://www.smartcret.com/en/blogs/news/problems-with-microcement-yes-they-exist-avoiding-them-is-easy))

**Exceptions (mass-waterproof products):**
- **Mortex** — water-repellent in the mass, often used without secondary membrane on stable substrates ([Mortex Beal](https://bealinternational.com/en/mortex/))
- **Tadelakt** — the lime + soap saponification creates calcium stearate that plugs the pores; period-correct for hammams and traditional baths

Even with mass-waterproof products, IL good-practice **still** uses a membrane for showers — because the failure is in joints and substrate movement, not in the microcement itself.

**Sealer renewal interval (critical and under-disclosed):**
- Dry areas — every 4–5 years
- Wet areas (showers, kitchens) — every 2 years ([microcementec problems](https://www.microcementec.com/en/microcement-problems-tips-to-solve-cracks-scratches-stains/))

### 2.7 Known issues — what fails and why

Synthesised from [Smartcret problems](https://www.smartcret.com/en/blogs/news/problems-with-microcement-yes-they-exist-avoiding-them-is-easy), [Topciment problems guide](https://www.topciment.com/en-us/new/microcement-problems-tips-to-avoid-them), [Seamless Overlays — cracking + discoloration](https://seamlessoverlays.com/blog/microcement-problems-cracking-discolouration-amp-how-to-avoid-them), [microcementec fissures](https://www.microcementec.com/en/fissures-in-microcement-causes-and-effective-solutions/):

| Failure mode | Root cause | How to prevent |
|---|---|---|
| **Hairline cracking** | Substrate movement; missing decoupling membrane; rigid microcement over flexible subfloor | Always use mesh + decoupling at joints; never apply over un-cured screed (≥ 28 d) |
| **Larger structural cracks** | Building settlement, no expansion joint respect | Honour every existing structural joint with a matching surface joint |
| **Colour drift / blotchy patches** | Inconsistent mix ratio between batches; high humidity during cure; uneven trowel pressure | Mix full bags only; control RH ≤ 60% during cure; same trowel hand for the whole panel |
| **Trowel marks visible** | Premature water evaporation, drying too fast | Cool, low-RH room; avoid direct sun/draughts |
| **Surface scratches in service** | Sealer wear; furniture without felt pads | Felt pads; renew sealer at correct interval |
| **Sealer milkiness / loss of gloss** | Sealer aged out, especially in wet area | Renew every 2 yr wet, 4–5 yr dry |
| **Pool / outdoor color loss** | UV + chlorine + salt | Spec UV-stable product (Atlanttic), UV-stable sealer |
| **Sealer peel in shower** | Inadequate cure time before water exposure | 7 d cure for PU in shower zones before first wet use |
| **Efflorescence (white bloom)** | Water pushing through from substrate, dissolving calcium hydroxide | Fix moisture ingress first; use vapour-permeable sealer only above-grade |

## SECTION 3 — CROSS-CATEGORY NOTES FOR THE ENCYCLOPEDIA

### 3.1 What both categories share
- Both are **seamless** — that's the design promise that brings customers to the FloorDSGN brief.
- Both **depend on substrate quality** — moisture, flatness, soundness. Skipping prep is the #1 failure root.
- Both have a **sealer / wear coat** that must be renewed on a schedule. Customers must be told this **up front**.
- Both can be specified with **R-class slip rating** for wet/outdoor areas (rubber via embedded aggregate; microcement via Atlanttic-type anti-slip granulometry).

### 3.2 What separates them
- **Rubber = elastic, energy-returning, acoustic.** Microcement = rigid, polished, photogenic.
- Rubber typically **8 mm – 40 mm** (sport-grade). Microcement typically **2–3 mm** decorative.
- Rubber wears slowly + invisibly. Microcement shows wear in the sealer/sheen first.
- Rubber comes from a **factory** (homogeneity guaranteed). Microcement is hand-applied — installer skill is decisive.
