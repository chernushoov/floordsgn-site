PU-Cement Flooring

Polyurethane-Cement Hybrid Systems

Polyurethane-cement hybrid systems engineered for extreme thermal cycling. From boiling water to freezer temperatures — without cracking. The industry standard for food and beverage processing.

Where PU-Cement Performs

PU-cement is the only flooring system specifically designed for the demands of food processing environments where thermal shock, aggressive cleaning, and hygiene requirements converge.

Food & Beverage Processing

Breweries, dairies, meat processing. Hot wash-downs, caustic cleaning, steam exposure. The industry standard.

Commercial Kitchens

Hot oil spills, boiling water, constant cleaning. Texture is selected for wet environments and cleaning method.

Pharmaceutical

Clean-in-place compatible. Withstands aggressive sanitization protocols.

Bakeries & Laundries

Hot oven interfaces, steam exposure, constant moisture, detergent resistance.

Built for Abuse

PU-cement systems are thick-build mortars that require proper drainage design and substrate preparation for optimal performance.

  • Falls to drain — Minimum 1:80 fall required. We install screeds to create proper drainage
  • Shot blasting — Aggressive profile (CSP 5-7) for mechanical bond to substrate
  • Coved skirting — Integral cove at wall junctions eliminates corner joints
  • Channel drains — Stainless steel drainage integrated into floor system
  • Moisture tolerance — PU-cement is moisture-tolerant but excess water should be addressed

System Architecture

PU-cement systems combine the strength of cement with the flexibility and chemical resistance of polyurethane.

01

Bonding System

Epoxy or polyurethane primer creates chemical bond to prepared concrete. Critical for adhesion under thermal stress.

02

PU-Cement Mortar

Polyurethane-bound cementitious mortar troweled to specified thickness. Contains special aggregates for thermal stability and wear resistance.

03

Seal Coat

Polyurethane topcoat seals surface, provides chemical resistance, and determines slip texture level.

Texture Options

Slip resistance is critical in wet food processing environments. We offer the full range of textures:

Smooth (R10)

Easy to clean, moderate slip resistance. Dry processing areas.

Medium (R11)

Balanced cleanability and grip. Most food processing applications.

Coarse (R12)

Higher slip resistance for wet/oily areas. Slightly more effort to clean.

Heavy Duty (R13)

Maximum grip for extreme wet conditions. Industrial drainage areas.

FAQ

Common Questions About PU-Cement Floors

Why choose PU-cement over epoxy?

PU-cement is specifically designed for environments with thermal shock — hot wash-downs, steam cleaning, freezer interfaces. Standard epoxy can crack under these conditions. If your facility requires daily hot water cleaning or has temperature extremes, PU-cement is the right choice.

Can PU-cement support food-safety audits?

PU-cement is commonly used in food and beverage environments because it can be detailed as a seamless, cleanable system with coves and drainage. Final compliance depends on the selected product, specification and site audit requirements.

How thick is a PU-cement floor?

Typically 6-9mm, significantly thicker than epoxy (2-5mm). This thickness provides impact resistance, thermal mass for temperature cycling, and allows for integral falls to drain. The thickness is a feature, not a limitation.

Can PU-cement be installed over existing floors?

Usually existing coatings need to be removed down to concrete. The system requires aggressive mechanical bonding and often needs screeding to achieve proper drainage falls. We assess overlay feasibility during site inspection.

How slippery is PU-cement when wet?

PU-cement comes in multiple texture levels. We specify grip based on water, grease, slope, drainage and cleaning method, because heavier texture improves grip but requires more cleaning effort.

What's the installation process for an operating facility?

We typically install in phases to minimize disruption. Weekend work is common. The system cures quickly (24-48 hours to traffic), so areas can return to production rapidly. Full project planning coordinates with your production schedule.
